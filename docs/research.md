# Lotline: evidence and research

Research checked 20 October 2026. Primary sources only. These findings establish a workflow worth investigating; they do not establish customer demand, traction, regulatory compliance, or market size.

## The specific user

An operations or quality lead at a small Canadian packaged-food manufacturer who already records supplier receipts, ingredient consumption, production batches, and customer shipments in spreadsheets. Initial discovery should focus on makers distributing across provincial boundaries and doing manual recall rehearsals. Restaurants are not the target: CFIA's traceability fact sheet expressly excludes restaurants and similar enterprises from these particular requirements.

## Evidence behind the workflow

1. **Traceability is a document-and-relationship problem.** CFIA describes one-step-back/one-step-forward records, lot identifiers, supplier/customer information and dates. Applicable records must be readable, retained for two years, accessible in Canada, and electronic records supplied in a usable single file. This supports a source-linked import/export workflow. It does not mean Lotline's hosting or export alone meets all requirements. [CFIA traceability fact sheet](https://inspection.canada.ca/en/food-safety-industry/toolkit-food-businesses/traceability)
2. **Recall rehearsal is recurring.** CFIA's inspection procedure checks recall simulations at least once every 12 months, with recorded details and results retained for two years, for applicable operators. This gives a concrete job: rehearse whether the records can identify relevant lots and distribution. Applicability varies; Lotline does not decide legal obligations. [CFIA inspection procedure](https://inspection.canada.ca/en/inspection-and-enforcement/guidance-food-activities/preventive-control-inspection/traceability-inspection)
3. **The output needs quantities and destinations, not prose alone.** CFIA's recall guide describes assembling information about affected food, codes, quantities and distribution. Ingredient and packaging relationships can matter. Lotline supports record preparation; a qualified human decides the actual scope and response. [CFIA recall procedure guide](https://inspection.canada.ca/en/food-safety-industry/recall-procedure)

## Existing alternatives

**Katana** already offers a Traceability Explorer with source relationships, quarantine quantities, and customer impact. A graph alone is not novel. Lotline's proposed distinction is importing the records a small maker already has and surfacing gaps before making confident claims. [Katana product documentation](https://support.katanamrp.com/en/articles/14737323-how-to-use-the-traceability-explorer)

**FoodDocs** already offers traceability and recall data in a broader food-safety system. Its pricing page lists Professional at $299/site/month or $250 billed annually; verify currency and current terms before using these in sales material. This is evidence of a paid category, not proof that buyers want Lotline. [FoodDocs pricing](https://www.fooddocs.com/pricing)

**Spreadsheets and consultants** are the default alternative to test in discovery. We have not established their typical costs, error rates, or dissatisfaction through interviews. Avoid invented time-savings statistics and unsupported market-size claims.

## Selection decision

Choose Lotline over a general RFP assistant or change-order assistant because the demo has a concrete trigger, inspectable source records, and a strong visual result. It also lets AI remain narrowly responsible for interpreting a file layout while deterministic code handles lineage and arithmetic. Differentiation and willingness to pay remain hypotheses.
